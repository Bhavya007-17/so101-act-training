## 0 · The question

Three ways of turning a natural-language instruction into robot motion, measured on the same tasks with the same success criteria:

1. **An LLM agent calling scripted primitives** — plans in language, acts through hand-written skills.
2. **ACT (behaviour cloning)** — no language input, learns one visuomotor policy per task from demonstrations.
3. **SmolVLA** — language-conditioned learned policy, the bridge between the two.

The deliverable is the honest shape of the trade-off, not a winner. If the learned policies lose on most cells, that is the result and it gets published as the result.

---

## 1 · The circularity problem, and how it is resolved

<aside>
⚠️

**The flaw, stated plainly:** if ACT is trained by behaviour cloning on demonstrations generated by the IK agent, and then evaluated against that same IK agent, the comparison measures **imitation fidelity, not learned capability**. Under behaviour cloning the student cannot exceed the teacher on the training distribution. Run naively, this benchmark is structurally incapable of producing the finding P1 exists to produce.

This was flagged on 2026-08-05 with no resolution recorded. It is resolved here, four ways.

</aside>

**Fix 1 — Change the axis. The sim comparison is privileged state versus pixels.**

The agent reads ground-truth object poses straight out of MuJoCo and grasps by weld constraint. ACT reads two camera images and joint state, and nothing else. The student is strictly information-poorer than the teacher. So the sim question is not "which is smarter" but **"how much of a privileged-state expert survives distillation into a pixels-only policy?"** — a real question, with a real literature behind it (teacher–student and privileged-information distillation), and not a circular one.

**Fix 2 — The expert is a ceiling, never an opponent.**

Every ACT number is reported twice: as an absolute success rate, and as **percentage of demonstrator performance retained** on the same seeds. Under behaviour cloning, 100 percent retention is the ceiling. Nobody gets to be surprised that the student did not beat the teacher, and no reader gets to be misled into thinking that was the test.

**Fix 3 — One task class the agent structurally cannot do.**

T4 (push-to-target, no grasp) has no matching primitive in look / find / pick / place. Its demonstrations come from a separate scripted push controller, **not** from the agent. On T4 the agent scores zero by construction, the data source is independent, and the comparison is genuinely non-circular.

**Fix 4 — The headline result is deferred to hardware, and August says so out loud.**

On the SO-101 the demonstrations are **human teleoperation**. The IK agent is not in the loop, does not exist on that platform, and cannot be the data source. Circularity disappears completely.

<aside>
📌

**Therefore: the August sim run is a toolchain validation and a distillation study. It is not the finding.** The finding arrives in November, on hardware, with human demonstrations. Anything published in August states this in its first paragraph. Publishing a sim number as though it were the headline would be exactly the overclaim P1 was created to correct.

</aside>

### Resolving the standing open question

> *"Is the MuJoCo voice arm formally the P1 benchmark baseline?"* — open on the Projects page since the database was built.
> 

**Decided: yes for the sim phase, as a privileged-state expert and as a data source, labelled as such everywhere. Conditionally for the hardware phase.**

The agent does not port to hardware for free: it depends on ground-truth object poses, which a real camera does not hand you. That dependence is itself a finding — the baseline's sim advantage is partly an artefact of information the physical world does not supply.

- **Gate:** if AprilTag markers on the four cubes plus a pose estimator are working by **2026-10-15**, the agent runs as a third arm on hardware.
- **Otherwise:** the hardware comparison is ACT versus SmolVLA only, and the agent's absence is **reported as a result about privileged-information dependence** — not quietly dropped as a condition.

## 2 · Systems under test

| Arm | What it is | Observation | Language input | Data source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — LLM agent** | NVIDIA NIM plus Gemini for ambiguous requests, local parser for common commands, calling look / find / pick / place. Differential IK via mink; weld-constraint grasping. | Ground-truth object poses (privileged) | Native | n/a — hand-written |
| **B — ACT** | Action-chunking transformer trained through LeRobot. One policy per task. | Two RGB streams (front three-quarter, wrist) plus joint state | None — structural | Demonstrations |
| **C — SmolVLA** | ≈450M-parameter language-conditioned VLA, LeRobot-native, fine-tuned on the same demonstrations as B. | Same as B, plus the instruction string | Native | Demonstrations |

Arms B and C see **identical data**. Any difference between them is architectural, which is the only reason that particular comparison is worth running.

**Why SmolVLA and not OpenVLA or π0:** compute is one 8 GB-VRAM laptop with no cloud budget. OpenVLA at 7B and π0 do not fit and were killed for that reason. SmolVLA is the largest language-conditioned policy that trains on the available hardware, and it is native to LeRobot, which the rest of P1 already depends on. Stated here so the model choice reads as a constraint that was reasoned about, not a preference.

## 3 · Task set — and why these four

Built from the scene that already exists and is already reproducible: four coloured cubes plus a shelf, external camera `front_34`, wrist camera at fovy 75. Each task isolates one axis of the prediction table; the fourth exists to break circularity.

| ID | Task | Axis it isolates | Why it is in the set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1** | Pick the red cube, place it on the shelf | Baseline competence | The canonical task. If nothing works here, nothing downstream means anything. Also the control condition. |
| **T2** | Place a cube on a marked target region, tight tolerance | Placement precision | The closest thing to contact precision that weld-constraint grasping permits. Explicitly a **proxy** — see the note below. |
| **T3** | "Put the blue cube on the shelf" with all four cubes present | Language grounding | Distractors force actual referential resolution. This is where ACT is structurally out of the running, and that is the point. |
| **T4** | Push a cube into a target region without grasping | Capability outside the primitive set | No primitive exists for it, and its demonstrations come from an independent controller. The one genuinely non-circular cell. |

<aside>
🚫

**Contact precision is not measurable in the current sim, and no attempt will be made to fix that in August.** Grasping is a weld constraint; there is no friction, no slip, no real contact. Retuning MuJoCo contact parameters is a well-known time sink and the hardware phase supplies real contact physics for free in eight weeks. T2 measures **placement error in millimetres** as a declared proxy, and the contact-precision column of the prediction table is settled on hardware, not here.

</aside>

## 4 · Evaluation conditions

| Code | Condition | Definition | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **C1** | In-distribution | Initial object poses sampled from the same 20 × 20 cm region as the demonstrations | T1–T4 |
| **C2** | Unseen positions | Poses sampled from a 30 × 30 cm region with the trained 20 × 20 cm centre held out | T1–T4 |
| **C3** | Unseen instructions | Ten paraphrase templates written and sealed before training, never shown to any arm | T3 only |
| **C4** | Unseen object | One cube colour held out of all demonstrations entirely | T1, T3 |

Evaluation seeds are disjoint from demonstration seeds and from the ten-seed development set used for any tuning. A seed fixes the initial object poses, distractor placement and robot start configuration, so **all three arms see byte-identical initial states.** The design is paired throughout.

---

## 5 · Metrics

**Primary**

- **Success rate**, binary per trial, per task per condition per arm. Reported with the trial count, always. A rate without its denominator is not a result.

**Secondary**

- **Placement error** — millimetres from target centroid, on successful trials only (T2 primarily).
- **Time to completion** — median seconds, and the interquartile range.
- **Control frequency achieved** — Hz, measured not claimed. For arm A also: decisions per episode and seconds of model latency per decision.
- **Retention** — ACT and SmolVLA success as a percentage of the demonstrator's success on the same seeds (see Fix 2).
- **Failure taxonomy** — every failed trial is assigned exactly one label: approach miss · grasp failure · dropped in transit · placement out of tolerance · **wrong object** · collision or safety abort · timeout · controller divergence. The distribution of failures is often more informative than the rate itself and is much harder to fake.

**Cost, tracked and published**

- GPU-hours per training run · tokens and dollars spent on arm A · hours of physical lab time. A benchmark that hides its cost is not reproducible by anyone else with a laptop.

---

## 6 · Success criteria — decided now, before any result

- **T1** — cube's centroid inside the shelf footprint, resting below rim height, gripper released, object stationary for 2 seconds. No other cube displaced by more than 2 cm.
- **T2** — cube centroid within **15 mm** of the target centre, stationary for 2 seconds.
- **T3** — the **instructed** cube satisfies T1, and no distractor cube is displaced by more than 2 cm. Moving the wrong cube is a failure even if the placement was clean.
- **T4** — cube centroid inside the target region, stationary for 2 seconds, gripper never closed at any point in the episode.
- **Timeout** — 60 seconds of simulated time or 1000 control steps, whichever comes first. Counts as failure, labelled as timeout.
- **Safety abort** — any self-collision or workspace-limit violation. Counts as failure.

A trial is scored by the automated checker, once, with no human adjudication and no re-runs. If the checker turns out to be wrong about something, the fix goes in the Deviations log and **every affected cell is re-run in full**.

## 7 · Trial counts, power, and the stopping rule

| Phase | Trials per cell | Detectable difference (95% Wilson, non-overlapping) | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sim (August) | 50 paired seeds | ≈ 25 points | Episodes are nearly free; 50 is the floor, not a target. |
| Hardware (Oct–Nov) | 20 trials | ≈ 30 points | Physical trials cost roughly 45 s each plus reset. A reduced grid of 2 tasks × 3 conditions × up to 3 arms is already 240–360 real trials, or 5–8 hours of bench time across several sessions. |

<aside>
📏

**The honesty clause that matters most.** At these trial counts, only large gaps are real. A difference will be **claimed** only where the 95 percent Wilson intervals do not overlap. Everything smaller is reported as **inconclusive** — not as a "trend," not as "suggests," not as "slightly better." Writing this down now removes the temptation to narrate noise in November.

</aside>

**Pre-committed extension rule (this is the whole defence against optional stopping):** if a pairwise difference in a sim cell is under 15 points with overlapping intervals, that **one** cell may be extended to 150 seeds for arms B and C. No other extensions. No cell is ever extended because the result was disappointing, and no cell stops early because the result was pleasing. Any extension is logged with its date.

## 8 · What is held fixed

- Same scene, same robot model, same controller interface, same control rate for all arms.
- Arms B and C: same demonstration set, same episode count, same preprocessing, same augmentation. **3 training seeds each**, reported as mean and standard deviation across seeds, each evaluated on the identical evaluation seeds.
- Demonstration budget fixed before collection begins and held to. The number is set the day collection starts and written into the Deviations log if it changes.
- **Arm A's prompt is frozen on the day evaluation begins.** Any edit afterwards invalidates that arm and forces a full re-run of every arm-A cell, logged. Tuning a prompt against the evaluation set is the single easiest way to fake this benchmark, so it is closed off explicitly.
- Evaluation seeds are never used for tuning anything. The 10-seed development set exists for that.
- The ten paraphrase templates for C3 are written and sealed **before** training starts.

<aside>
🧪

**Declared limitation, sim phase.** Scripted-expert demonstrations are cleaner and more consistent than human ones. ACT trained on them will look better in sim than it will on hardware, where the demonstrations are human, noisy, and multimodal. This is stated up front rather than discovered as a surprise in November, and it is a second reason the sim numbers are not the headline.

</aside>

## 9 · Pre-registered predictions

**Written 2026-08-06, before any run.** Reporting where these turn out wrong is the strongest possible version of this writeup — considerably stronger than a clean win.

### The shape

| System | Language generality | Contact precision | Speed | Runs without privileged state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LLM agent** | High | Low | Slow | **No** |
| **ACT** | None — one policy per task | High | Fast | Yes |
| **SmolVLA** | Moderate — the bridge | Medium | Fast | Yes |

The fourth column is the addition that makes the comparison non-circular. Plain ACT is not language-conditioned, so it cannot compete on unseen instructions at all — **that is the finding, not a flaw**, and it is what motivates SmolVLA existing in the set.

### The numbers (hardware phase, success rate)

| Cell | LLM agent | ACT | SmolVLA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 · in-distribution | 75% | 80% | 65% |
| T1 · unseen positions | 70% | 55% | 50% |
| T2 · precise placement | 30% | 65% | 40% |
| T3 · seen phrasings | 80% | 25% *(structural)* | 60% |
| T3 · unseen phrasings | 70% | 25% *(structural)* | 40% |
| T4 · push, no grasp | 0% *(structural)* | 55% | 35% |

*Structural* means the number follows from architecture, not performance. ACT has no instruction input, so on T3 it selects whatever it was trained to select and scores at chance across four cubes — 25 percent. Arm A has no push primitive, so T4 is 0 percent. Both are reported as architectural facts and neither is presented as a defeat.

**Latency predictions:** arm A at 3–12 s per decision, 0.1–0.3 Hz effective replanning · ACT at 30–50 Hz on the laptop · SmolVLA at 5–15 Hz raw, roughly 30 Hz effective once action chunks execute open-loop.

## 10 · Where I expect to be wrong

Ranked by how likely the prediction is to break. Recorded now so that being wrong is a finding rather than an embarrassment.

1. **SmolVLA may simply not converge.** ≈450M parameters, 8 GB of VRAM, and a demonstration set one student can collect by hand. Perhaps a one-in-three chance it lands under 20 percent on every task. If so, the honest finding is *"small VLAs need more data than a single operator can produce,"* which is a genuinely useful result and directly reshapes the winter phase.
2. **ACT on unseen positions may hold up far better than 55 percent** if the demonstration distribution covers the workspace more broadly than expected. The 55 is a guess with wide error bars around it.
3. **Arm A's speed penalty may mostly vanish** if it plans once and executes open-loop, in which case "slow" is measuring planning latency rather than task time — and the metric, not the system, was wrong.
4. **T2 may not discriminate at all** if 15 mm turns out to be either trivially easy or impossible for everything. Tolerance is the parameter most likely to need a documented revision after the first pilot.

## 11 · Analysis plan

- Wilson 95 percent intervals on every reported rate. No bare point estimates anywhere.
- **Exactly two confirmatory tests**, both McNemar on paired trials, α = 0.05: **(i)** ACT versus SmolVLA on T2 · **(ii)** arm A versus SmolVLA on T3 unseen phrasings. Two planned tests, no correction, stated up front.
- Everything else is **exploratory** and labelled as exploratory in the writeup, including every cross-task comparison and the entire failure taxonomy.
- Failure distributions reported as raw counts per label per cell.
- Retention percentages reported for arms B and C throughout the sim phase.
- Cost table published alongside results.
